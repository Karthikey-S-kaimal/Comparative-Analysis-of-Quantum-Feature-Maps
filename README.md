# Comparative Analysis of Quantum Feature Maps

## ZFeatureMap vs. ZZFeatureMap vs. PauliFeatureMap on the Iris Dataset

A simulation-based quantum computing project investigating how different quantum feature maps affect **circuit complexity, feature interactions, entanglement, transpiled circuit complexity, and measurement distributions**.

The project uses the **Iris dataset** and Qiskit to compare:

* ZFeatureMap
* ZZFeatureMap
* PauliFeatureMap

It also includes a foundational exploration of different approaches for encoding classical data into quantum states.

---

## Research Question

> **How does the choice of quantum feature map affect circuit complexity, feature interactions, and quantum data representation?**

---

## Project Objectives

* Study how quantum feature maps transform classical features into quantum states.
* Compare ZFeatureMap, ZZFeatureMap, and PauliFeatureMap.
* Analyze circuit depth and gate complexity.
* Study the role of two-qubit gates and entanglement.
* Compare original and transpiled circuits.
* Observe differences in measurement distributions for different Iris classes.

#  Feature Map Comparison

The main experimental study compares three Qiskit feature maps.

### 1. ZFeatureMap

A relatively simple feature map based on single-qubit phase operations.

Characteristics:

* No two-qubit gates
* Low circuit depth
* Low gate count
* No entanglement generated by the feature map

### 2. ZZFeatureMap

Extends the encoding by introducing pairwise interactions between features using ZZ interactions.

Characteristics:

* Two-qubit interactions
* Greater circuit complexity than ZFeatureMap
* Significant entanglement
* Represents pairwise feature interactions

### 3. PauliFeatureMap

Uses Pauli operators to construct a more general feature map with single- and multi-qubit interactions.

Characteristics:

* Higher circuit complexity
* Two-qubit interactions
* Significant entanglement
* More complex quantum data representation

# Results

## Original Circuit Complexity

| Feature Map     | Depth | Total Gates | 1-Q Gates | 2-Q Gates |
| --------------- | ----: | ----------: | --------: | --------: |
| ZFeatureMap     |     4 |          16 |        16 |         0 |
| ZZFeatureMap    |    31 |          52 |        28 |        24 |
| PauliFeatureMap |    45 |         108 |        84 |        24 |

The results show a clear increase in circuit complexity as richer feature interactions are introduced.

---

## Meyer–Wallach Entanglement

The ZFeatureMap produces approximately zero global entanglement because it does not contain two-qubit entangling operations.

Representative entanglement values for the other two feature maps were:

| Iris Class | ZZFeatureMap | PauliFeatureMap |
| ---------- | -----------: | --------------: |
| Setosa     |       ~0.756 |          ~0.734 |
| Versicolor |       ~0.459 |          ~0.435 |
| Virginica  |       ~0.848 |          ~0.914 |

This demonstrates that the amount of entanglement is influenced not only by the feature-map structure but also by the input data.

---

## Transpilation Results

| Feature Map     | Original Depth | Transpiled Depth | Original 2-Q Gates | Transpiled 2-Q Gates |
| --------------- | -------------: | ---------------: | -----------------: | -------------------: |
| ZFeatureMap     |              4 |                5 |                  0 |                    0 |
| ZZFeatureMap    |             31 |               30 |                 24 |                   20 |
| PauliFeatureMap |             45 |               34 |                 24 |                   20 |

Transpilation reduced the two-qubit gate count for both entangling feature maps.

# Measurement Distribution Analysis

The three feature maps were executed with **4000 shots** for representative samples from the three Iris classes:

* Setosa
* Versicolor
* Virginica

The resulting measurement distributions differed across the feature maps.

This demonstrates that different feature maps can produce substantially different quantum representations even when the same classical input data is used.

The corresponding plots are available in the `results` section of the project.

---

# Key Findings

The experiments reveal a **complexity–interaction trade-off**.

### ZFeatureMap

* Simplest circuit
* Lowest gate count
* No two-qubit gates
* Approximately zero entanglement
* Lowest computational circuit cost

### ZZFeatureMap

* Introduces pairwise feature interactions
* Uses two-qubit gates
* Generates significant entanglement
* Moderate circuit complexity
* Provides a balance between interaction and circuit cost

### PauliFeatureMap

* Highest original circuit complexity
* Uses two-qubit interactions
* Generates significant entanglement
* Produces richer feature transformations
* Requires greater circuit resources

### Overall Conclusion

There is no universally best feature map.

The choice depends on the desired balance between:

```text
Circuit Complexity
        ↕
Feature Interaction
        ↕
Quantum Representation
```

Increasing feature interactions generally comes with increased circuit cost.
